There are three types of data transmission, known as modes, used in network devices. They describe the direction in which data can travel between two devices.

## Simplex: ##
The Simplex data transmission mode allows one-way communication, which means data travels in only one direction. One device sends data, and the other device only receives it.

For example: A TV station sends signals to televisions, and the televisions simply receive them without sending anything back.

## Half-Duplex: ##
The Half-Duplex data transmission mode allows two-way communication, but not simultaneously. Both devices can send and receive data, but they cannot transmit at the same time.

Example: Walkie-talkies.

One person speaks while the other listens. After the first person finishes transmitting, the other person can respond. Both devices must take turns transmitting data.

## Full-Duplex: ##
The Full-Duplex data transmission is a two-way simultaneous communication. Both devices can send and receive data, and are also able to transmit at the same time.

A few examples: Video/audio calls and computer and switch can transmit and receive data simultaneously over a full-duplex link.
